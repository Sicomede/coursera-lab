# Business Analysis and Process Management: CraftVerify

A guided business analysis project that decomposes a hypothetical computer-vision-based skill verification platform for skilled trades into stakeholders, processes, responsibilities, and data flows using standard BA techniques and draw.io diagrams.

## Prompt

The project asks the learner to take a business concept and apply core business analysis and process management techniques to it. The deliverables include a business overview document modeled on the Mahi's Styling Business example, a stakeholder analysis, a process flow diagram, a BPMN-style swimlane diagram showing actor responsibilities, a RACI matrix for the key process tasks, and a data flow diagram showing how information moves through the system. The goal is to demonstrate that a business idea can be decomposed into stakeholders, processes, responsibilities, and data flows in a way that another analyst could pick up and continue.

## What I built

A complete business analysis package for CraftVerify, a hypothetical platform that uses computer vision to grade work samples from skilled trades workers (welders, electricians, plumbers, tile setters) and turns those graded samples into portable, worker-owned reputation portfolios. The package includes six artifacts: a business overview document, a stakeholder power and interest grid, a process flow diagram, a two-phase BPMN swimlane diagram, a RACI matrix across twelve tasks and six actor roles, and a level 1 data flow diagram showing five processes connected by four data stores. All diagrams are draw.io exports rendered for dark mode.

## Skills covered

- Business overview documentation
- Stakeholder analysis using power and interest grids
- Process flow modeling with decision gateways
- BPMN 2.0 swimlane notation including start events, end events, exclusive gateways, sequence flows, and message flows
- RACI matrix construction enforcing one accountable role per task
- Data flow diagram construction with external entities, processes, and data stores
- Identifying handoff points between actors as integration risk surfaces
- Recognizing feedback loops in process models (the data flywheel pattern)

## What I learned that I'll actually use

- A power and interest grid is more useful than a flat stakeholder list because it tells you what to do with each stakeholder, not just who they are.
- BPMN swimlanes turn process flows into accountability diagnostics. Each arrow crossing a lane is a handoff and therefore a failure point worth scrutinizing.
- A RACI matrix with multiple As on the same task is an organizational design problem disguised as a documentation problem. The single-A rule forces clarity.
- Data flow diagrams answer different questions from process flow diagrams. Process flows tell you what happens; data flows tell you what information must persist and where. Both are needed for system design.
- Splitting complex BPMN flows into phases is acceptable and often better than cramming everything onto one canvas.
- The data flywheel pattern (sample feeds the training dataset which improves the model which scores the next sample better) is a reusable structural pattern, not specific to this domain. Same shape as Tesla autopilot data collection.

## Business overview: CraftVerify

### Overview

CraftVerify is an online platform that provides computer-vision-graded skill verification for workers in the skilled trades. Welders, electricians, plumbers, tile setters, and other craftspeople photograph or upload short videos of their actual work samples (weld beads, panel installs, pipe sweats, tile cuts) and receive an objective quality score generated by computer vision models. Verified samples build into a portable portfolio that workers carry across jobs and present to contractors, homeowners, insurers, and unions during hiring.

### Target consumer profile

**Primary user (worker side):**
- Age range 22 to 45
- Apprentice through journeyman skilled-trades worker
- Lower to middle income, with significant earning potential tied directly to demonstrable skill
- Often union-affiliated or transitioning between contractors
- Takes pride in craft quality and wants the market to see it

**Consumer classification:**
- The journeyman electrician or welder who knows he's better than his current rate reflects, looking for evidence to bring to a contractor negotiation. Power users. Refer other tradespeople.
- The recently certified apprentice trying to break into competitive markets without a long word-of-mouth network. Needs the portfolio to substitute for years of jobsite relationships.
- The independent contractor who wants to charge premium rates and needs third-party verification to justify them.

**Secondary user (buyer side):**
- Age range 30 to 60
- General contractors, insurance underwriters, union apprenticeship coordinators, residential homeowners hiring for renovations
- Pays for access to the verified pool, or receives liability discounts for hiring from it

### Strategic goal and vision

To become the trusted infrastructure for verified trades reputation in North America, replacing word-of-mouth and paper credentials with portable, objective, worker-owned proof of craft quality. Long-term: the default thing a contractor checks before making an offer, the way a recruiter checks LinkedIn.

### Status of the business

The business is a startup in the prototype validation phase. A single-trade pilot (welding) is being scoped to test whether contractors actually consult verified portfolios during hiring decisions. Market research is complete; technical proof of concept for CV-graded weld bead scoring is the next milestone.

### Competition

CraftVerify has no direct competitors building CV-graded skill verification for physical craft work. Indirect competition is significant and includes:

- Union credentialing systems (which certify completion of programs but not work quality)
- LinkedIn and similar professional networks (which capture self-reported skill but no verified samples)
- Trade-specific certification bodies like AWS for welding (which test under exam conditions but do not provide ongoing portfolio infrastructure)
- Contractor referral platforms like Angi and Thumbtack (which surface reviews but not work quality)
- The NABTU and Microsoft training partnership (substrate-adjacent but focused on AI training delivery, not skill verification)

The closest adjacent player is Skillit, which is positioning in construction trade tech but is not building CV-graded portfolios.

### Business needs

- Computer vision engineering team with experience in industrial inspection models
- Partnerships with one or two trade schools or union locals for initial training data
- Reliable cloud infrastructure for video and image processing at scale
- Insurance industry advisor to develop the liability-discount channel
- UI/UX designer with experience designing for mobile-first blue-collar users
- Initial marketing focused on welder forums, trade subreddits, and union newsletters
- Legal counsel on worker-owned data and portability commitments

### Skill verification business process steps

1. The worker downloads the CraftVerify app and creates a profile selecting their primary trade.
2. The worker is prompted to submit a sample of recent work, either a photograph or a short video, following on-screen guidance for angle, lighting, and reference scale.
3. The submission is uploaded and processed by the trade-specific computer vision model, which evaluates the sample against quality dimensions appropriate to that trade. For welding this includes bead consistency, penetration evidence, and spatter patterns. For tile work this includes grout line uniformity and cut precision.
4. The worker receives a quality score with a breakdown across the assessed dimensions, plus visual annotations on the sample showing what the model evaluated.
5. The worker chooses whether to publish the scored sample to their public portfolio. Unpublished samples remain private and do not affect their public profile.
6. Tier-dependent path:
   - Free tier: up to ten verified samples and basic portfolio sharing.
   - Professional tier: unlimited samples, contractor-facing analytics showing who viewed their portfolio, and integration with major trade job boards.
   - Union or enterprise tier: portfolio integrates with apprenticeship records or employer roster, and supervisors gain dashboard access to track skill development across the team.
7. Buyers (contractors, insurers, unions) access verified worker pools through a separate search interface that surfaces workers by trade, geography, and minimum score thresholds.
8. After a hire happens, the worker is prompted to log the engagement, building a track record alongside the verified samples. Buyers can rate jobsite reliability separately, keeping the CV-verified craft score and the human-rated reliability score as two distinct signals.
